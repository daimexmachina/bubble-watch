## 0. The problem this plan solves

Two things emerged from the literature review and reconnaissance that the current model gets wrong
or misses entirely.

**0.1 Two measured correctness bugs.** The leveraged indicator understates risk by a measured
**15.1 stress points**, for two independent reasons:

- **ORCL is reported as having no debt.** `LongTermDebt` resolves to a *stale 2022 value of zero* for
  Oracle, and the code takes the first resolving tag, so ORCL's $122.3B of notes
  (`LongTermNotesPayable`) is invisible. Oracle is the single most-exposed company in the cohort by
  every other measure, and the model currently scores it as debt-free.
- **Operating leases are excluded.** `OperatingLeaseLiability` resolves for all five filers.
  Including it moves the cohort mean from 0.556 → 0.955 (x operating cash flow), i.e. stress 18.2 →
  33.3.

**0.2 The model measures prices, not financing structure.** Its credit indicators are *public* HY/IG
index spreads. The BIS (WP 1367) and IMF (GFSR Apr 2026, Fig. 1.18 "Artificial Intelligence
Circularity") both locate this cycle's fragility in **debt and circular equity ties**, which the tool
cannot see. Meanwhile the composite says "early" while the BIS models over-investment at ~1.5x the
efficient level. That divergence is not necessarily an error — different questions — but a reader
must not receive "early" without it.

## 2. Phase 1 — integrity (correct what is wrong)

### 1.1 Leverage correction
- Fix the ORCL zero-debt bug: resolve across candidate tags and reject a stale/frozen series.
- Include `OperatingLeaseLiability`; add `UnrecordedUnconditionalPurchaseObligationBalanceSheetAmount`
  where disclosed.
- **GUARD:** an absent tag renders as *"not disclosed"*, **never 0.00x**. MSFT has no purchase
  obligation tag; printing zero would be a fabricated value.
- Re-anchor for the new ratio's range; methodology → 1.2.
- **Verify:** live score moves as predicted; ORCL appears with real leverage.

### 1.2 Depreciation / useful-life subsidy (Kshirsagar-Chen)
- Indicator: annual depreciation ÷ gross PP&E, trended; a *falling* rate while PP&E grows means
  useful lives are being extended, which flatters earnings.
- **GUARD (critical):** a naive implementation produces **3 false positives out of 5**. META's gross
  PP&E is *frozen at $56.43B* across three years (stale tag) and AMZN's jumps 3.3x in one year (a
  reporting break). Rule required: require the series to be present for N consecutive periods and
  reject single-period jumps beyond a threshold — emit a gap, not a number.
- **Verify:** ORCL 8.99% → 6.22% reproduces; the other four correctly report not-measurable.

### 1.3 RPO vs revenue
- The strongest new candidate and the only one that touches the circularity gap, because a deal with
  a company you funded appears here as contracted backlog.
- **BLOCKED:** GOOGL's rank rests on a 242.8B → 467.6B single-quarter jump. Must establish whether
  that is real contract wins or a definitional change before it ships.
- Be genuinely critical: a high RPO/revenue may be a healthy forward book, not a bubble signal.

### 1.4 GSADF explosive-root test (Phillips-Shi-Yu)
- The academic standard for detecting and **dating** exuberance; already applied to AI specifically
  (Sarkar & Wells 2026). Needs only a price series, which the tool already fetches.
- Rationale: the tool currently ships only a weighted average. The literature converges on
  multi-method diagnosis. Reporting "composite: early; exuberance test: not significant" is far more
  informative than one average, and lets two methods agree or disagree in the open.

### 1.5 Literature citations
- Cite BIS WP 1367, BIS Bulletin 120, IMF GFSR Apr 2026, NBER w34722, Kshirsagar-Chen, Van
  Nieuwerburgh in the config reasons and declared gaps, so a reader can see the basis is not the
  author's priors.

### 1.6 Per-company exposure report *(new — user request)*
Answer two questions in every report: **who is most exposed**, and **who might have issues first**.

- Ranked table using the measured ratios above.
- Explain *why* the ranking differs from the intuition: near-term debt is small everywhere, so the
  exposure is in unconditional commitments and revenue concentration, not refinancing dates.
- **GUARDS:** MSFT's absent tag renders *not disclosed*; META has no by-year breakdown so the tool
  must say it cannot state *when* the $279B falls due; GOOGL's RPO/revenue is held until 1.3 clears.
- Must state plainly that a large backlog is not self-evidently bearish.

**Phase 1 done when:** all corrections live and probe-verified, the guards are test-covered, the
composite has moved for stated reasons, `cargo test` green, methodology bumped to 1.2 with the
comparability guard refusing cross-version baselines, committed and pushed.

## 3. Phase 2 — financing architecture (the BIS/IMF thesis)

The structural gap. Scope depends on the re-dispatched private-credit agent's verified findings.

- **Private credit** — Fed Z.1 has explicit series (~$1.539T). Currently invisible to the tool.
- **Off-balance-sheet exposure** — SPVs, JVs, sale-leasebacks. Van Nieuwerburgh: *"the user of compute
  is not the owner of the underlying assets."*
- **Maturity/commitment timing** as a first-class dimension rather than a level.
- **Credit history fix** — add `BAA10Y` so credit has 4 decades instead of 2.5 years, making the
  indicator meaningful against the 2000 and 2008 episodes.

## 4. Phase 3 — falsification

**The tool currently cannot show the bubble thesis is wrong.** Every indicator accumulates
confirming evidence only. Wang & Chen's five-pillar framework and Caballero's "temporary
overvaluation can leave a permanent real legacy" both point here. Deliverable: at least two
indicators designed to *disconfirm*, plus an explicit statement in the report of what would change
the verdict.

## 6. Standing rules for all phases

1. **Never print a number the source did not supply.** An absent tag is *"not disclosed"*, not zero.
   A stale series is a rejection, not a reading.
2. **State the direction of every bias.** If a gap makes the score look calmer than reality, say so.
3. **Measure before modelling.** Every anchor must be justified by a real measured value.
4. **Methodology version bumps on any redefinition**, and the archive refuses cross-version baselines.
5. **A large number is not automatically bearish.** RPO/revenue and backlog concentration cut both
   ways; the report must say so.
