## Task 1 — OpenRouter token-volume source · DONE (`7cee2ac`)

`src/sources/openrouter.rs`. 16 unit tests.

Three bugs found by running it against live data rather than trusting fixtures:

1. **The "7-day window" is not seven complete days.** 573 rows, but only ONE date fully populated
   (544 rows, 1.27e14 tokens); the other six carried 1-10 rows and as few as 27 tokens. Reading the
   newest date would report a demand collapse that never happened. Guard: `latest_complete_day()`.
2. **The newest week is partial.** 5.8e10 against 1.29e14 — a ~2,200x artefact. Unguarded, the
   growth calculation returned **"0.0x over a window in which usage grew 24.5x."**
   Guard: `drop_partial_trailing_week()`, with a test proving a credible week is KEPT.
3. **Open-weight share read 2.3% against a true ~70%.** The rankings feed keys on `canonical_slug`
   (date-suffixed); the catalogue's `id` omits it. Joining on `id` matched almost nothing — a wrong
   answer that looked like a real measurement. Both keys now indexed; 165/499 unmatched slugs carry
   1.38% of tokens, so the share is reported as an INTERVAL (73.8-74.4%), not a point estimate.

My fixtures had been written to match my GUESS at the weekly shape; every fixture test passed and it
failed on the live endpoint with "no `data` array". The real shape nests: `data.data[].{x, ys}`.

## Task 2 — `inference_demand` indicator · DONE (`8e80658`)

Weight 10, methodology 2.2 → 2.3, total weight 158 → 168. Live: **+176.3% over 13 weeks, stress 26.8,
contribution 1.60.** Offline: reported GAP, coverage drops 100% → 89% (never a zero).

**A calibration bug found on the first live run.** I used the longest available lag (51 weeks) with
anchors derived from the 13-week distribution. Live, that scored **+2,351% against a scale topping
out at +400%** — clamped to the top anchor, so the indicator could distinguish nothing from anything.

| lag | observations | range |
|---|---|---|
| 13 weeks | **39** | +3.5% … +385.1% |
| 26 weeks | 26 | +318.2% … +739.3% |
| 51 weeks | **1** | +2,351.4% |

A 51-week lag has exactly ONE observation, so any scale built on it has one point. Now calibrated at
13 weeks. Trade stated in the rationale: a quarter detects deceleration later than a year would, and
it is the longest lag this series can support a calibration for.

Guard tests also caught two things I had missed: the missing plain-English name, and the stale weight
total in README.
